# Version 3.0.4

Version 3.0.4 fixes numerical stability, training gradients, mining boundaries,
and retrieval edge cases. Public signatures and minimum supported Python,
PyTorch, and NumPy versions are unchanged.

## Numerical fixes

- `SNRDistance` computes low-precision differences and variances in float32,
  preventing small nonzero variances from becoming NaN distances and corrupting
  `SignalToNoiseRatioContrastiveLoss` gradients
  ([#790](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/790)).
- `LpRegularizer`, `ZeroMeanRegularizer`, and `SphericalEmbeddingRegularizer`
  retain float32 statistics and nonempty scalars for low-precision inputs,
  preventing overflow before reduction or later regularization weighting
  ([#791](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/791),
  [#795](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/795)).
- `LargeMarginSoftmaxLoss` and `SphereFaceLoss` widen magnitude calculations
  before scaling logits, preserving representable values and gradients
  ([#792](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/792)).
- `ManifoldLoss` evaluates ranking terms in log space to keep large finite
  margins from overflowing
  ([#793](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/793)).
- ArcFace losses retain target-cosine derivatives in the linear continuation
  and at zero margin. `DynamicArcFaceLoss` also preserves power-law margins
  for extreme powers and closely spaced integer class counts
  ([#794](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/794),
  [#805](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/805)).
- `MultiSupConLoss` preserves caller-supplied matrices with greater precision
  than the embeddings
  ([#797](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/797)).
- NCA, ProxyNCA, DCL, and DCLW retain loss differences beside large common score
  offsets. Centered scaling also handles opposite finite extremes whose raw
  difference overflows
  ([#799](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/799),
  [#807](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/807)).
- `RankedListLoss`, `SoftTripleLoss`, and `NormalizedSoftmaxLoss` preserve
  representable results through extreme temperature scaling. SoftTriple
  supports both temperature signs; NormalizedSoftmax training continues to
  use the public `get_logits` extension hook
  ([#800](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/800),
  [#803](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/803),
  [#804](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/804)).
- PNP variants retain very small penalties and gradients instead of losing
  them to cancellation
  ([#801](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/801)).
- `IntraPairVarianceLoss` widens pair statistics, `P2SGradLoss` forms cosine
  residuals before low-precision rounding, and `CircleLoss` widens adaptive
  weights and centers its log partitions
  ([#808](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/808),
  [#810](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/810),
  [#811](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/811)).
- Shared normalization preserves directions and gradients for finite large
  embeddings. Dtype and shape bounds skip rescaling when the norm cannot
  overflow; P2SGrad uses the same normalization
  ([#812](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/812)).

## Mining and retrieval

- Weighted triplet sampling rescales eligible weights before converting to
  float32, preserving relative probabilities for tiny or huge finite weights
  ([#796](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/796)).
- BatchEasyHardMiner and BatchHardMiner keep finite dtype-limit scores inside
  the candidate set. BatchEasyHardMiner applies inclusive distance ranges to
  the `"all"` strategies as well
  ([#798](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/798),
  [#813](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/813)).
- `DistanceWeightedMiner` widens density arithmetic so high-dimensional,
  low-precision inputs do not silently lose eligible anchors
  ([#806](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/806)).
- Empty retrieval queries work consistently with self-search, custom label
  comparisons, NDCG, and per-class reductions
  ([#802](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/802)).
- NDCG uses shared gain scaling to preserve ranking ratios for large and
  subnormal relevance gains
  ([#809](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/809)).

## Observable changes

- Nonempty low-precision losses from the regularizers listed above,
  RankedListLoss, IntraPairVarianceLoss, NormalizedSoftmaxLoss, and CircleLoss
  retain float32 through reduction. Gradients still accumulate in the input
  and parameter dtypes; empty reductions retain their existing behavior.
- **Exact seeded weighted triplet draws can change.** Relative sampling
  probabilities are preserved.
- NormalizedSoftmax normalizes class weights once. Tiny class weights therefore
  follow the distance's documented epsilon without a second normalization.

The [integration measurements](https://github.com/KevinMusgrave/pytorch-metric-learning/pull/814)
compare loss-only forward/backward workloads on an A100. The largest measured
float32 NT-Xent case (batch 1,024, dimension 512) costs about 0.34 ms and 2 MiB
more for overflow-safe normalization. Proxy classification improved; results
depend on the workload and do not measure a complete training pipeline.
